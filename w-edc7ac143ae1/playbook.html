A walkthrough for notified bodies

Reading a REALM dossier

Conformity assessment of medical AI is normally performed on documentation supplied by the manufacturer. This walkthrough covers the same assessment performed differently: every figure in the dossier is computed by the assessor's own infrastructure, on patient data that never leaves the hospital.

What a notified body is asking

  1. Is this the device that was submitted? Identity and provenance.
  2. Could this evidence answer the question? Data fitness and statistical power.
  3. Do the declared claims hold? Independent adjudication with confidence intervals.
  4. Will degradation in service be detected? Post-market surveillance.

REALM provides a screen for each of these questions, and this walkthrough covers them in that order. Every image is a capture from a running node. Each chapter opens with narration written to be read aloud, followed by the screen it describes and the observations an assessor should take from it.

Chapter one

The review queue

/reviews

Narration

This is the review queue. Each entry is a manufacturer's submission whose evidence is already recorded on the ledger. Before a reviewer opens it, the node has computed the performance metrics itself, and compared them against the claims the manufacturer declared.

The verdict shown is therefore the node's own finding, not the manufacturer's assertion reprinted. That is the substantive difference from conventional conformity assessment, where the figures in the dossier are the ones the manufacturer chose to report. The reviewer's decision, to accept or to reject, is taken from this screen, and accepting it validates the card on the ledger.

Observation

The queue applies no owner filter. A notified body on this node sees the submissions of every manufacturer, because the reviewer is a node-level role rather than a per-customer one. This is the correct model for a federated node, and it carries an operational consequence: confirm the token number before accepting, since the adjacent entry may belong to a different manufacturer.

Chapter two

Is this the device that was submitted?

/models/4 · provenance and certification

Narration

Identity is established before performance. A performance figure means nothing unless the artefact that produced it can be identified, so the node certifies the container image before it will run any evaluation against it. It performs that certification with the network disabled, so the artefact under test cannot reach an external service while it is being measured.

Five gates are reported separately: conformance, behaviour, determinism, hermetic execution, and a signed vendor attestation. The attestation is verified against a public key the node enrolled in advance, so the image content, identified by digest, matches what that key signed. That is what ties this assessment to one specific build rather than to a version number, which is the precision an audit actually needs.

The determinism gate runs the model five times and confirms the output is stable across a container restart. The restart condition is the one that carries weight. A model can be perfectly reproducible inside a single process and still return different answers after a restart, through an unpinned seed or non-deterministic kernel selection. Such a model would pass this assessment and then diverge in service, with nothing downstream able to detect it.

A performance figure is not interpretable unless the artefact that produced it can be identified, which is why this screen precedes the evidence. The image reference, its digest and the signing key are recorded on the card, so an assessment can be tied to a specific build rather than to a version number.

Observation

The determinism gate reports runs: 5, restart and stable. The restart condition is the substantive one. Observation

The closing line of the panel states that the software bill of materials is recorded for information only and never gates certification. The interface therefore identifies which of its own indicators is not a gate. That is a useful property when assessing the tool itself, since a system that marks the boundary of its own assurance provides a reasonable basis for accepting its other indicators.

Chapter four

Do the declared claims hold?

/cards/7 · performance

Narration

The manufacturer declared two performance claims. The first is on AUROC, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, which measures how well the model ranks patients who go on to develop acute kidney injury above those who do not. They claimed at least zero point seven five. The node measured zero point seven nine, with a bootstrap confidence interval of zero point seven six to zero point eight one, on sixteen thousand eight hundred and twenty- nine patients the manufacturer cannot access.

The second claim is on AUPRC, the area under the precision-recall curve. The difference between the two matters here. AUROC treats both classes alike and is largely indifferent to how common the outcome is, whereas AUPRC considers only the patients the model actually flags, so it becomes harder to score well on as the event gets rarer. In this cohort acute kidney injury occurs in eighteen point eight per cent of patients. The claim was at least zero point four zero, and the node measured zero point four nine. Both claims are met.

Two further figures deserve attention. Sensitivity is zero point two three and specificity is zero point nine seven, so at the operating point supplied this device identifies fewer than one in four of the patients who go on to develop acute kidney injury, while almost never raising a false alarm. That is a coherent setting for prioritising a worklist and an unsuitable one for a safety net, and which of the two is intended is a question about the intended-use statement rather than about the model. A dossier reporting AUROC alone would not have exposed it.

Permutation importance shows creatinine accounting for more of the model's performance than every other feature combined. Two things follow from that. The model is sensitive to a single site changing assay or units. And because acute kidney injury is defined by creatinine movement, it is worth asking how much of that importance is prediction and how much is the outcome definition entering the inputs. Each subgroup also reports its own sample size and event count, so the best-looking figure on the screen, zero point eight zero for patients under forty, is visibly the one resting on the fewest events.

Both declared claims are adjudicated against measurements taken on the node, on a cohort the manufacturer cannot inspect. The remainder of the panel carries the detail that determines how much the headline figures are worth.

Observation

AUROC is 0.79. Sensitivity is 0.23 and specificity is 0.97. At the operating point supplied, the device identifies fewer than one in four patients who subsequently develop acute kidney injury, and raises very few false alarms. That configuration is consistent with worklist prioritisation and inconsistent with use as a safety net. Which of the two applies is a question about the intended-use statement rather than about the model, and a dossier reporting AUROC alone would not expose it.

Chapter five

Will degradation in service be detected?

/cards/7 · post-market drift monitoring

Narration

The device was certified at a discrimination figure of zero point seven nine. This table shows the twelve months that followed, as monthly windows of deployed performance scored against the deployment baseline. The device did not change. The population it was applied to did.

Three signals are monitored independently, because a model can fail on any one of them alone: discrimination, calibration, and input distribution shift. April is flagged as a change point and then recovers, with May through August all clear. From September onward the alerts are sustained, and by November discrimination has fallen to zero point six zero. The distinction matters more than any single month: one alerting window is not evidence of degradation, whereas a sustained run is, and that is the difference between an alert a reviewer acts on and one a team learns to ignore.

The three signals also cross their thresholds at different times. Discrimination degrades from September, calibration only in October, and input distribution shift never crosses its threshold at all, peaking at zero point two four seven against a limit of zero point two five. That last one is the consequential one, because input distribution shift is the only one of the three that can be computed before outcomes are known, and therefore the only warning available in real time. In this series it would have reported nothing for eleven months, which tells you that early warning is weak here and confirmation is late.

Alert thresholds are a discrimination drop above 0.05 from baseline, expected calibration error above 0.10, and a population stability index above 0.25. A window raises an alert when any one of the three is exceeded.

Observation

April raises an alert and then recovers: May through August are all clear. September onward does not recover. A single alerting window is not evidence of degradation; a sustained run is. That distinction determines whether an alert is actionable, and it is not available from a surveillance obligation discharged as one annual figure.

Observation

The three signals cross their thresholds at different times. Discrimination degrades from September. Expected calibration error crosses 0.10 only in October. The population stability index never crosses its threshold, peaking at 0.2467 in December against a limit of 0.25.

That last point has practical consequences. Chapter six

Human oversight as structured fields

/cards/7 · EU AI Act Art. 14

Narration

Article fourteen of the AI Act requires that a high-risk system be subject to effective human oversight. On this card the requirement is recorded as structured fields rather than prose: the oversight model, who performs it, the intended environment of use, what override means in practice, the fallback when the system is unavailable, and the measures taken against automation bias. The difference from a paragraph of narrative is that each field is a discrete statement for which supporting evidence can be requested.

There is a boundary worth stating plainly. The card asserts these measures. The node does not verify that the deployed interface implements them. Structured metadata makes the claim assessable; it does not make it verified.

What is real and what is not

Narration: closing

To summarise what is real. The model is a trained, signed and certified artefact. The pre- market cohort is real clinical data. Every metric, confidence interval, conformance check and claim verdict was computed by the same code that runs a live evaluation.

The post-market surveillance dataset is synthetic. The arithmetic in that table is real. The twelve months it runs over are manufactured.

Finally, REALM is not a designated body and does not issue certificates. It produces evidence for an assessment that a notified body still signs. Where an independent estimate contradicts a declared claim, the system will not validate the card silently, but it will permit a reviewer to overrule the adjudication with a recorded rationale. The objective is not to automate the judgement. It is to make the judgement answerable.

Disclosure belongs at the start of the conversation rather than in the questions afterwards. An assessor who identifies an undisclosed synthetic dataset partway through will discount the material already presented.
