The four questions it answers

Is this the device that was submitted? Could this evidence answer the question? Do the declared claims hold? And will degradation in service be detected? REALM has a screen for each, and the walkthrough covers them in that order, ending on the record of the decision itself.

What is real and what is not

Stated up front rather than in the questions afterwards, because an assessor who finds an undisclosed synthetic dataset partway through will discount everything already shown.

Real
The model: a trained artefact, containerised, signed and certified by the node's own factory.
Real
The pre-market cohort: 16,829 patients, the manufacturer's own 30% hold-out, converted from MIMIC-IV into OMOP on the node. Evaluating on it reproduces the declared performance under the node's control; it is not external validation.
Real
Every metric, confidence interval, subgroup result, conformance check and claim verdict, produced by the code that runs a live evaluation.
Synthetic
The post-market surveillance dataset, 6,000 dated records. The drift arithmetic is real; the twelve months it runs over are manufactured.
Synthetic
The narration voice, from a neural text-to-speech model run locally. The script is written to be read by a person.
Not claimed
REALM is not a designated body and does not issue certificates. It produces evidence for an assessment that a notified body signs.
